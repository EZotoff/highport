# Highport — Concept

> The main terminal of your Traveller campaign.

Highport is an open-source companion for **Mongoose Traveller 2nd Edition** (MGT2E) campaigns. It begins where Traveller is most distinctive — in the shipyard of character creation — and grows with your table into a living campaign memory, a ship's intelligence that speaks to your crew, and eventually a sector that breathes while you sleep in jump space.

This document is the canonical product vision. It is organized as three tiers, each building on the one before it. The tiers are not separate products — they are stages of a single tool that grows from a single character's service record into the politics of an entire sector.

For staging and delivery status, see [ROADMAP.md](./ROADMAP.md). For system design, see [docs/architecture/](./docs/architecture/).

---

## The Three Tiers

| Tier  | Name                | Premise                                                                              | Status                                                                           |
| ----- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A** | Character Creation  | Turn procedural Traveller chargen into a collaborative, replayable backstory engine. | In progress — mechanics near-complete, AI integration live, gamification pending |
| **B** | Campaign Management | Carry accepted characters and lore into a gated, voice-aware campaign memory.        | Foundations in place — RAG + knowledge scopes + portraits shipped                |
| **C** | World Simulation    | Model factions, resources, and goals so the sector moves between sessions.           | Research stage — architecture only                                               |

The **Command Deck** metaphor runs through all three:

- **The Manifest** (Tier A) — personnel files, service jackets, medical records.
- **The Intercom** (Tier B) — the voice of the ship: logging the present, answering the crew, guarding what they have not earned.
- **The Astrogation Tank** (Tier C) — the holographic sector projection where borders bleed and distant fleets maneuver in the dark.

## Tier A — Character Creation as Collaborative Backstory Engine

### Why Traveller chargen is the seed

Traveller's lifepath system is unlike any other RPG: you don't _choose_ a backstory, you _survive_ one. Each four-year term rolls survival, events, skills, and advancement; mustering out delivers cash and benefits; connections tie you to other player characters through shared history. A fresh character arrives already scarred, employed, discharged, decorated, or broken — with decades of implied life behind them.

The procedural output is a **skeleton** — a sequence of mechanical facts:

> Characteristics 777755. Navy, three terms, Rank 3 (Lieutenant). Event: _Betrayed by a friend or contact._ Mishap: _Severely injured in action. Medical bills paid. Mustered out._ Skills: Pilot-1, Gunnery-2, Leadership-1. Connections: Ally (PC), Rival (NPC).

The skeleton is mechanically complete and gameable. But it is not yet a person.

### The skeleton/meat contract

Highport's central idea is that **AI layers "meat" onto the procedural "skeleton" Traveller generates** — but never overrides the skeleton itself. The dice own the facts; the AI owns the texture.

- **The skeleton is immutable.** Characteristics, career path, event triggers, skill tallies, benefit rolls, rank, aging — these are produced by the MGT2E rules engine and cannot be rewritten by AI. Provenance is always `mgt2e-rule`.
- **The meat is drafted.** Names, faces, _why_ a career was chosen, full event scenes with named NPCs, mishap recovery arcs, _how_ a skill was learned, the acquisition story of a benefit, the felt dynamics of a connection — these are generated by the AI and remain **draft** until a player or GM accepts, edits, or rejects them. Provenance is `ai` until accepted.

Every piece of generated content carries a provenance record:

```
{
  source: "ai" | "player" | "gm" | "mgt2e-rule",
  mode: "brief" | "inspiration" | "full",
  status: "draft" | "accepted" | "rejected" | "edited",
  derivedFrom: { characterId, termNumber, eventId, rollResult }
}
```

This contract is what makes the system trustworthy: you always know what the dice produced, what the AI proposed, and what you chose to keep.

### AI invasiveness — three modes

Each generation surface (event descriptions, NPC details, connection suggestions, and later campaign artifacts) supports three invasiveness levels, set per session:

| Mode          | Public name     | What it produces                                                                                                         | Risk                                  |
| ------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| `brief`       | **Brief**       | A 1–2 sentence gloss that names the event and moves on. Low draft load; player fills in the rest.                        | Minimal                               |
| `inspiration` | **Inspiration** | Several concrete hooks or options the player picks from. Suggestions only; nothing is canon until selected.              | Low                                   |
| `full`        | **Full**        | A coherent scene with named NPCs, persona cues, and relationship implications. Draft canon requiring accept/edit/reject. | Higher — more to review, more to gain |

> **The modes are a dial, not a switch.** A table can run an entire campaign on Brief and never feel the AI's presence, or dial to Full for pivotal events and let the AI draft scenes that the player then shapes. The default is Inspiration — enough to spark, never enough to crowd out the player's voice.

### A worked example

The skeleton:

> Valen "Rook" Toris. STR 7 DEX 7 END 7 INT 7 EDU 7 SOC 5. Navy, Term 3. Event: _Betrayed by a friend or contact._ Mishap: _Severely injured in action. Medical bills paid. Mustered out._ Medical discharge, Rank 3.

The same skeleton, three modes:

**Brief** —

> During your third Navy tour, a trusted contact sold out your patrol route. The resulting ambush left you with a shattered femur, a medical discharge, and a score to settle.

**Inspiration** — pick one:

- _The Sabotaged Drive._ An engineering mate bypassed radiation shielding to run contraband; you took the rad-burst meant for him.
- _The Ambush._ A Vargr corsair knew your sensor cycle. Your CO was the leak. You were the casualty.
- _The Fall Guy._ You discovered supply skimming. A "friend" in logistics arranged an airlock malfunction.

**Full** —

> The betrayal didn't come with a dramatic speech; it came as a silent sensor-blind spot over the ice-moon of Keng. Lieutenant Aris, your academy bunkmate, had sold the patrol schedule to the Oghman raiders. When the corsair's torpedo breached the port hull, the bulkhead crushed your left leg before the emergency seals engaged. You spent three months in a med-tank on a naval hospital ship, staring at the ceiling while the Navy quietly paid your medical bills and handed you a discharge paper to sweep the scandal under the deck plating. Aris got a promotion. You got a limp.

Same skeleton. Three intensities of meat. The player decides how much AI texture they want layered onto the dice.

### The gamification aspiration

Today the flow is linear: roll, read, advance. The long-term goal is a character creation experience with the **replayability and texture of a game like Tyranny** — where each term feels like a chapter, where the scrapbook of accepted scenes builds a service record you want to revisit, and where the AI's invasiveness feels like a co-author offering ideas rather than a narrator taking over. This is a North Star, not a v1 feature. The mechanics and the AI integration ship first; the gamified framing follows.

## Tier B — Campaign Management as Living Memory

### From character sheet to campaign canon

When a character is accepted out of chargen, they cross a threshold: from a draft into the campaign's **canon**. From that point on, the character, their connections, their history, and the lore they touched become part of a shared, persistent, knowledge-gated memory — the campaign's living record.

Tier B is the system that holds that memory, answers questions about it, generates new creative material within it, and — crucially — **knows what each person at the table is allowed to know.**

### Knowledge gating — who knows what

The campaign memory is partitioned by scope:

| Scope       | Who can see it   | Example                                                                     |
| ----------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `public`    | Anyone           | The Trojan Reach is a border sector. The Aslan Hierate lies spinward.       |
| `party`     | The player group | "The crew of the _Harrier_ has a Letter of Marque from the King of Drinax." |
| `gm`        | GM only          | "Duke Oleb owes GeDeCo 50 MCr in secret gambling debts."                    |
| `char:<id>` | One character    | A personal journal entry, a private debt, a hidden agenda.                  |

The default is `public`. Material is promoted into more restricted scopes deliberately. The ship's AI (below) can only ever answer from scopes the querying player is permitted to see — **scope is filtered before retrieval, not by the prompt.** The AI persona is not a security boundary; the scope filter is. (Current implementation filters scopes correctly but trusts a client-supplied header to determine identity; a server-side authenticated resolver is planned before serious multi-user deployment. See [ROADMAP.md](./ROADMAP.md) Known Issues.)

> **Near-term stance:** treat all players as sharing `public` + `party`. Per-character secrets (`char:<id>`) are a target, not a v1 promise. The architectural seams are in place; the resolver matures over time.

### The Ship's AI — player-facing

The player-facing assistant is **diegetic**: it is the crew's ship computer, speaking in character. It answers from ship's logs, public universe lore, and party-known campaign memory. It does not know what the GM hasn't let it know.

Default persona: **The Steward** — modelled on the Jeeves archetype (P.G. Wodehouse): unflappable, discreet, quietly indispensable. It anticipates needs before they are spoken, registers disapproval of ill-considered plans through exquisite politeness, and maintains the same measured tone whether reporting a blown fuse or a hull breach. Formal diction, dry wit, never pedantic. In-universe, it is the senior steward of a gentleman's club who has been reassigned to a tramp freighter and is making the best of it. The discretion is not merely character — it is the diegetic face of the knowledge gate. The Steward genuinely does not know what it has not been permitted to know; it is not coached to hide things, because the scope filter runs before the prompt is ever built.

A player asks the ship about a vessel on sensors:

> **Steward (player-facing):** "I have taken the liberty of consulting the sensor logs, sir. The vessel appears to be a Harrier-class, though her transponder suggests otherwise — a subterfuge, I fancy. Given your previous encounter at Theev, this is in all probability the _Void-Dancer_. I ought to mention that our hull integrity stands at sixty-four per cent. I would not presume to dictate the captain's course of action, but I confess I find the prospect of an engagement... inadvisable."

What the Steward did _not_ say (because the GM has not revealed it): the _Void-Dancer_'s jump drive is disabled, she is carrying 20 dtons of stolen Imperial platinum, and her crew is desperate enough to surrender at the first shot. That lives in `gm` scope. The Steward is genuinely ignorant of it — not coached to hide it.

### The GM's AI — omniscient prep assistant

The GM-facing assistant is **non-diegetic** and omniscient within the campaign's permissions. It sees GM-only records, proposes secrets, summarizes factions, generates NPC dialogue and prep material, and explains inconsistencies the players haven't noticed yet. It is a co-GM, not a character.

Same sensor contact, GM view:

> **GM Assistant:** "The _Void-Dancer_ is currently crippled (Jump Drive disabled) and carrying 20 dtons of stolen Imperial platinum. They are desperate and will surrender if fired upon. Want me to draft Captain Vel's surrender terms, or the GeDeCo bounty claim the players could file afterward?"

Two AIs. Same backend. Different scopes, different personas, different jobs.

### Asset generation — making the canon tangible

Each accepted character or NPC can accrue generated assets:

- **Portraits** — shipped, Gemini-backed, with remix. Inherits entity visibility.
- **Personas** — structured profiles (voice, demeanor, secrets, speech patterns). Required before voice or social AI is reliable.
- **Voice** — text and cached TTS first; live voice is a hard latency/cost problem and is treated as experimental. The system will never promise real-time speech it cannot deliver.
- **Theme clips** — async generation, never blocking the UI.

All assets inherit their entity's visibility. GM approval is required before a player-visible NPC gains secret-layer assets.

### Foundry VTT integration — scope, not replacement

Foundry VTT is the table's tactical runtime: token movement, grid combat, actor sheets, dice. Highport does not replace it. The integration is scoped to what Highport is good at — **narrative and campaign intelligence**:

- **In scope:** bidirectional actor sync for accepted MGT2E characters; push of NPCs and relationships into Foundry; social-scene context piped to the AI (active NPCs, relationships, public/party facts, GM-only motives for the GM assistant); suggested NPC dialogue, tactics, and emotional reactions; optional GM-approved "speak as NPC."
- **Out of scope:** grid combat automation, tactical combat decisions, token movement AI, rules adjudication, autonomous NPC actions without GM control.

The dividing line is simple: **Highport is the narrative and campaign intelligence layer; Foundry is the tactical tabletop and actor-sheet runtime.** Foundry handles the grid; Highport handles the conversation.

> The integration is currently **experimental**. The architecture is designed for bidirectional typed sync; runtime maturity is being proven.

## Tier C — World Simulation: The Living Sector

### The promise

While the crew spends two weeks in jump space, the sector does not hold its breath.

> On the dust-world of Pourne, the Glorious Empire quietly moved three strike cruisers into orbit, choking off the primary iridium export route. The local GeDeCo factor, starved of raw materials, slashed bounty payouts, forcing independent mercenaries to seek work with the very pirates the crew had been hunting. By the time the _Wayfarer_ translated back into real-space, their lucrative escort contract was already obsolete — replaced by a war they didn't start.

Tier C is the system that makes this possible: a sector that changes between sessions according to faction goals, resource flows, and the cascading consequences of player action — with the GM always in the loop.

### What it models

- **Factions** — leaders, resources, territories, and relationships. Aslan clans, Sword Worlds, megacorps, petty kingdoms, pirate fleets.
- **Resource ledgers** — credits, ships, troops, trade goods, influence, intel, logistics. A faction cannot mobilize a fleet it cannot feed.
- **Goal hierarchies** — strategic goals, decomposed into objectives, operations, and tasks. Updated dynamically as circumstances shift.
- **A decision engine** — rule-first proposal generation (deterministic scoring of candidate moves), stochastic variation (the universe is not perfectly predictable), and LLM narrative explanation (why the faction did what it did, in voice).
- **A resolution engine** — applies costs, risk, opposition, and consequences. Operations fail; fleets are intercepted; alliances fray.
- **An immutable event log** — every faction move is recorded. The GM can audit, rewind understanding, and explain _why_ the sector is the way it is.
- **A GM review queue** — major faction moves surface for approval, editing, or rejection before they become canon. The GM is the final editor of the living world.

### What it does not do

- It is **not autonomous.** The sector proposes; the GM disposes. AI proposes tactics, explains decisions, and flavors consequences — it does not unilaterally rewrite the world.
- It does **not start as a galaxy simulator.** The minimal viable Tier C is a single faction turn: snapshot state → generate candidate objectives → score with deterministic rules → LLM explains and proposes tactics → apply operation → surface to GM for approval → publish to the graph and RAG. From that seed, complexity grows.

> Tier C is the long horizon. It is named here so the architecture of Tiers A and B does not paint it out. Nothing in the near-term roadmap depends on it shipping, but everything in the near-term roadmap is designed to leave room for it.

## Design Principles

Inherited from the project's design context and extended for the product vision:

1. **Immersive utility.** The interface should feel like operating a starship bridge console — retro-sci-fi, industrial, data-dense, dark-mode, high-contrast — while never compromising the speed live tabletop play demands.
2. **The dice own the facts; the AI owns the texture.** Procedural skeletons are immutable. AI output is always draft until accepted. Provenance is always traceable.
3. **Knowledge is gated before retrieval, not by prompt.** Scope filtering is the correct boundary. The AI persona is a UX layer, not a fence. (Current enforcement uses client-trusted headers; a server-side authenticated resolver is the target.)
4. **Local-first by default, cloud-capable by choice.** The stack runs on free local providers (Ollama, ChromaDB) out of the box for text generation, embeddings, and vector storage. Cloud providers (Gemini, Pinecone, OpenAI) are opt-in for those who want them. Portrait generation currently requires a cloud provider (Gemini); local image generation is a future goal.
5. **The GM is the final editor.** Every AI output — chargen scene, NPC persona, faction move — can be accepted, edited, or rejected. Nothing becomes canon without a human's word.
6. **Honest scope.** The docs say what ships, what is experimental, and what is research. Voice and live world simulation are hard problems; we do not promise them before they work.

## The Path Forward

- **Tier A** is the immediate target: complete the gamified chargen experience, formalize the skeleton/meat contract, and make the three invasiveness modes feel like a co-author.
- **Tier B** builds on accepted canon: harden knowledge gating into a server-side resolver, ship the Ship's AI and GM Assistant personas, mature asset generation, and prove the Foundry integration at runtime.
- **Tier C** remains in architectural research: the faction turn engine, the resource ledger, and the goal hierarchy are designed for but not yet built.
